## The non-monotonic dip — why 0.20 loses more than 0.30

This is the key result. Both 0.20 and 0.30 trigger the **same** recurring fatal basket (Jul 16 11:45 LONG, 4-arm unanimous BUY at 0.92216 — the one that has fired in every 2024 run). The emergency cap fires at 35% of equity, so the **dollar loss is essentially the same** at both lot sizes:

| | v6 (0.20) | v5 (0.30) |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency date | Aug 5 08:52 | Jul 31 04:30 |
| Fire price | 0.89549 | 0.90049 |
| Total basket lots | 48.20 (L6) | 72.30 (L6) |
| Net pips at close | −161.7 | −111.7 |
| ≈ Dollar loss | ~$588 | ~$609 |

The smaller 0.20 lots let the basket fall **deeper** (more pips, 0.89549 vs 0.90049) before hitting the 35% cap — but the **realized dollar loss is the same ~$590-610** because the cap is a % of equity, not a function of lot size.

**So why does 0.20 end $130 lower?** Because the emergency loss is fixed (capped %) but the **winning baskets' profit scales with lot size**. At 0.20, the ~117 winning baskets across the year earn only ~⅔ of what 0.30's baskets earn per pip. Same emergency damage, weaker recovery → worse net.

**The structural lesson**: once a lot is big enough to trigger the unavoidable emergency, going *smaller* doesn't help — you eat the same capped loss but collect smaller wins. 0.20 sits in a **valley**: too big to dodge the emergency (like 0.10 does), too small to recover well (like 0.30 does). **It is a dominated option.**

## What this confirms about the safe ceiling

The v5 analysis predicted the true safe lot — the largest with **zero** 2024 emergencies — is **~0.15–0.17** (`0.10 × 35 / 20.3 ≈ 0.172`). v6 confirms the upper bound:

- **0.10**: worst 2024 basket floats to 20.3% DD → stays under cap → **0 emergencies** → +7.96%.
- **0.20**: same basket would float to ~40% DD → **crosses the 35% cap** → 1 emergency → −12.88%.

So 0.20 is already over the line. The safe ceiling is **below 0.20**, consistent with the ~0.17 estimate. Anything ≥ ~0.17 eats the Jul-16 emergency.

## Verdict — skip 0.20; the safe lot is ~0.15

v6 settles the 0.20 question: it's the worst fixed lot tested. It crosses the emergency threshold (so it pays the full capped loss) yet under-recovers versus 0.30. The recommendation stands:

- **Conservative / proven safe → 0.10** (zero emergencies both years, +$296 over 2 years).
- **Higher variance, accept a capped down year → 0.30** (one recoverable emergency, bigger easy-year upside, +$284).
- **0.20 → avoid** (dominated by both).
